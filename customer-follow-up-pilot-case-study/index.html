Illustrative customer follow-up workflow

Fictional example only. This page explains a possible workflow and measurement plan. It describes no real customer, completed pilot, or measured result.

An example problem

Imagine a service business where staff rely on memory and repeated manual checks to keep track of customer inquiries. During busy periods, it may become difficult to see who should respond next. This would be a starting hypothesis to investigate with the staff.

What to understand first

For an actual engagement, Workbuilt would review authorized examples and learn where inquiries arrive, who handles them, how handoffs work, and which tools and constraints matter. The business and its employees would help confirm the problem before choosing a change.

A possible small change

A shared, staff-reviewed follow-up queue could give each inquiry an owner, a next action, and a visible status. This is one possible approach. The appropriate improvement would depend on the business's actual process, evidence, and needs.

How a pilot could be evaluated

Before testing a change, the business and Workbuilt would agree on a limited scope, required permissions, and useful measures. Those could include staff time spent checking inquiries, response delays, and missed follow-ups.

A small, reversible test would use the same measures before and after the change. Staff feedback would help explain whether the workflow was useful and what needed adjustment.

Results remain to be established

This example has no measured customer outcomes. Any claim of saved time, fewer errors, or faster responses would need evidence from an actual, authorized pilot. Customer stories would also require permission before publication.

Deciding what comes next

An implementation decision would depend on the observed results, practical delivery requirements, an agreed scope and price, and the customer's approval. Optional continuing support would need its own agreed purpose and terms.
